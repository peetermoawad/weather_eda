🌍 Target Location Characteristics

Alexandria is Egypt's second-largest city and primary seaport, situated along the Mediterranean coast at 31.2001°N, 29.9187°E.

Part 2 • Data Quality & Engineering Zero-Data-Loss Architecture

Data Cleaning, Imputation & Sanity Audits

Transforming nested API responses into a clean, strictly indexed time-series dataset.

🧹 Step-by-Step Cleaning Pipeline

  • Temporal Indexing: Parsed strings formatted as YYYYMMDD into explicit pandas DatetimeIndex, sorted chronologically, confirming native daily frequency (freq='D').
  • Sentinel Missing Value Flagging: NASA POWER uses -999.0 as an internal sentinel fill value. We replaced all occurrences with np.nan.
  • Dual Imputation Strategy:
    • Continuous Physical Metrics (Temp, Humidity, Wind): Imputed via Linear Interpolation to respect natural thermal inertia.
    • Episodic Metrics (Precipitation): Imputed via Forward-Fill to strictly avoid inventing synthetic rainfall events.
  • Domain Sanity Bounds: Verified that all data points fall within physically realistic meteorological thresholds.

Part 2 Speaker Notes: "Data quality is the bedrock of any credible time-series analysis. When handling satellite-derived reanalysis data, a critical step is detecting sentinel values—in NASA's case, minus 999.0.

We designed a domain-aware dual imputation strategy: linear interpolation for continuous thermodynamic variables like temperature and humidity to preserve continuous physical transitions, and forward/backward filling for rainfall to avoid fabricating rain on dry days.

We ran strict unit and boundary assertion tests, ensuring zero negative precipitation and physical temperature plausibility across all 730 days."

Part 5 • Signal Decomposition Additive Model (s = 365)

Classical Time-Series Decomposition

Isolating Observed signal into Trend, Annual Seasonality, and Residual components.

🧩 Additive Model: Y(t) = T(t) + S(t) + R(t)

  • Observed Series: The raw daily mean temperature showing combined annual oscillations and daily turbulence.
  • Trend Component: The long-term moving trend remains flat between 21.5°C and 22.2°C across both years, confirming the absence of strong secular warming over this short 2-year horizon.
  • Seasonal Component (period = 365): A strong, deterministic annual sinusoidal curve with a seasonal amplitude of approximately ±7.5°C around the mean.
  • Residual Component: After removing trend and seasonality, residuals fluctuate symmetrically around 0 with no visible cyclic structure, resembling white noise with occasional storm perturbations.

🎯 Modeling Implication: The dominant power in temperature is deterministic seasonality. Models incorporating Fourier harmonic terms or SARIMA seasonal differencing will capture most of the variance.

Part 6 • Statistical Testing ADF & Autocorrelation

Stationarity Diagnostics & ACF/PACF

Augmented Dickey-Fuller hypothesis testing and autoregressive memory profiling.

🧪 Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) Tests

Series ADF Stat p-value Stationary?
Raw Mean Temperature -1.8959 0.3340 NON-STATIONARY ❌
Daily Precipitation -5.9701 0.0000 STATIONARY ✅
Temperature (1st Diff) -6.7376 0.0000 STATIONARY ✅

📉 ACF & PACF Diagnostic Signatures

  • Temperature ACF: Extremely slow decaying correlation (r > 0.95 at lag 1, continuing strong past lag 30). This is the hallmark signature of non-stationary seasonal persistence.
  • Temperature PACF: Drops precipitously after lag 1 (and minor lag 2), falling within the 95% confidence bands. This indicates an AR(1) or AR(2) structure after seasonal differencing!
Figure 6: Autocorrelation (ACF) & Partial Autocorrelation (PACF) Lags 1–30 Click to zoom 🔍

Part 6 Speaker Notes: "Now to stationarity—a pivotal requirement for time-series forecasting. The raw temperature series failed the Augmented Dickey-Fuller test with an ADF statistic of -1.89 and a p-value of 0.334, confirming it is non-stationary due to strong annual seasonality.

However, after taking the first difference, the ADF statistic plunges to -6.74 with a p-value less than 0.0001, confirming stationarity. Precipitation, due to its pulse nature, is naturally stationary (ADF = -5.97, p < 0.0001).

Furthermore, the PACF on Figure 6 cuts off abruptly after lag 1, indicating that an AR(1) process with seasonal differencing will be exceptionally effective."

Part 8 • Anomaly Detection Rolling Z-Score & IQR

Extreme Events & Outlier Diagnostics

Distinguishing real meteorological extreme weather events from sensor/API data corruption.

🔍 Dual Detection Methodology

  • 30-Day Rolling Z-Score (|z| > 3.0):
    • Temperature: 0 outliers detected. Daily temperatures move continuously within local monthly envelopes.
    • Precipitation: 18 anomaly days detected. Identifies acute flash storm pulses relative to localized rolling baselines.
  • Global IQR Fences (Q1 - 1.5×IQR to Q3 + 1.5×IQR):
    • Flags 133 rainfall days as statistical outliers because the 75th percentile is only 0.05 mm.
    • Crucial Conclusion: These points are valid physical storms, NOT data artifacts. Removing them would destroy storm predictability!
Top Flagged Events:

• 2023-04-12: Late-spring storm (11.71 mm, Z = 4.97)
• 2023-09-12: Rare early-autumn cloudburst (0.50 mm vs. 0.00 baseline, Z = 5.26)
• 2023-11-19: Heavy convective downpour (1.98 mm, Z = 5.09)

Part 8 Speaker Notes: "Anomalies in time series can stem from sensor malfunction or real atmospheric extremes. We cross-validated using two methods: a 30-day rolling Z-score and global IQR fences.

For temperature, zero outliers were flagged—confirming excellent data continuity. For precipitation, the rolling Z-score highlighted 18 days, such as the unusual April 2023 spring storm of 11.7 mm.

Because all records satisfied physical bounding assertions, we verified that these are genuine meteorological phenomena rather than API transmission glitches, meaning they should be retained for training extreme-event alert models."
